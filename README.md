SSDs won the operating-system battle years ago, but HDDs still win on one thing: price per terabyte. If you’re storing a 4K video archive, a Plex library, security footage or a backup target, a spinning drive is the most economical way to hold many terabytes.

Who still needs an internal HDD?

NAS and home-server builds
Game libraries (with the OS on an SSD)
Media and photo archives
Surveillance recording
Backup targets

The spec that trips people up: CMR vs SMR

CMR writes tracks separately. SMR overlaps tracks to fit more data, so changing data means rewriting neighboring tracks. For occasional copies that’s fine. During long writes, RAID rebuilds or heavy sustained use, SMR drives can slow sharply.

Rule of thumb: choose CMR for a NAS, RAID, or anything written constantly. SMR is acceptable for a single desktop drive used as cold storage. Recording method can differ between capacities in the same product family, so check the exact model’s datasheet.

Picks worth knowing

WD Red Plus 8TB (WD80EFPX): quiet, cool, CMR, built for RAID. Slower than 7,200 RPM peers.
Seagate IronWolf 4TB (ST4000VN006): low power and good value for small NAS builds.
WD Red Pro 8TB (WD8005FFBX): 7,200 RPM for larger arrays and heavier workloads, at a higher cost per TB.
Seagate BarraCuda 8TB (ST8000DM004): cheap bulk storage, but SMR, so it isn’t for RAID.

What to check before you buy

Form factor: 3.5-inch drives hold more and cost less per TB. 2.5-inch drives suit laptops and small builds.
Interface: most consumer drives use SATA III. SAS is for servers and needs a compatible controller.
RPM: 7,200 RPM seeks faster but runs hotter and louder. For a quiet living-room NAS, lower-RPM drives are often enough.
Capacity: 8TB–16TB usually gives the best cost per TB. Very small drives cost far more per TB.

Protect your data

All hard drives eventually fail. A drive is not a backup, and RAID protects against a drive failure but not against deletion, ransomware or a failed controller. Follow the 3-2-1 rule: three copies, on two types of media, with one off-site.

The sensible setup for most PCs is an SSD for the OS and apps plus an HDD for bulk storage. Prices are estimates and change often, so check the live listing.
